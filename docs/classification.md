# Classification

**In short:** your community decides who may see what. The kit carries those decisions
through everything it builds, so restricted knowledge reaches the people it belongs to
and nobody else.

This is the design. It isn't built yet.

## Why it's part of the model

Some knowledge is for certain people only, and that can include words and sentences as
well as stories. Old records often mix restricted and open material on the same page.
So classification can't be a filter added at the end. It has to travel with every piece
of material from the start.

## Your tiers, your rules

Your community sets up its own levels of access, which the kit calls tiers. The kit
comes with none of its own. For each tier you say:

- its name,
- which other tiers its readers may also see, and
- who may decide that something belongs in it.

Tiers don't have to form a ladder. Two tiers can each see the open material and be
closed to each other.

Material nobody has read yet isn't treated as open. It waits, unused, until someone has
read it and decided its tier. A computer search for likely restricted words can raise a
flag, but it can't clear anything.

The kit keeps a note of who classified each thing, when, and whether they read it. A
classification an AI suggested stays a draft until a person your community trusts
accepts it.

## Starting from a standard

You don't have to design your tiers from scratch. The kit can start from a published
scheme, such as the Traditional Knowledge Labels from Local Contexts. It drafts a set of
tiers, credit and terms from the labels you choose, and you accept, change or drop each
part.

If you already have tiers, you can say which labels each one matches. The kit then shows
those labels on everything it publishes, and recognises them on material that comes
from elsewhere. If material arrives with a restricting label that none of your tiers
matches, the kit holds it back until someone decides.

If your community already uses the Local Contexts Hub, the kit can bring in your labels
and the text you wrote for them.

## Every piece carries its tier

A whole recording can have one tier and a passage within it another. A word can be open
while one of its meanings is restricted. Something can be more restricted than what
contains it, but never less.

## A model for each tier

Each tier can have a full model of its own, with its own words, meanings, endings and
sentence patterns, kept in that tier's own store. A restricted tier can add to the open
model. It can, for example, add a word that takes the usual verb endings. It can't change
the open model or take anything away from it.

## Each build is for one audience

When the kit builds tables, practice or anything else, it builds for one audience at a
time and reads only what that audience may see. The open build is checked to make sure
it comes out exactly the same whether or not any restricted material is present. If it
doesn't, something is leaking, and the kit reports it.

Anything built from restricted material is restricted. A word form built from a
restricted word is restricted, even if every ending on it is open.

## Checking open material

To know whether something is safe to share openly, you need someone who knows the
restricted material. But what they know mustn't leak back with their answer. The kit
keeps the two apart.

**Screeners.** For each restricted tier, your community names the people who check open
material for it. If two tiers are closed to each other, like men's and women's
knowledge, each has its own screeners, and they work separately.

**Only a verdict comes back.** A screener looks at the material in their own space and
sends back one of three answers: clear, hold back, or "someone with authority needs to
look". The answer says which part of the material it's about. It doesn't say why, which
word, or what the concern is. The screener's notes stay with their tier.

**Fixing what's held.** Because the person who asked doesn't learn why something was
held, the screener's side suggests a replacement, and the replacement is checked again
before it goes back. If there's no good replacement, the part is left out.

**With computer help.** A computer can make a first pass. It runs where the restricted
material is kept, checks against that tier's lists, and can only say "hold back" or
"needs a person". Only a person who has read it can clear something.

If your community allows AI for a restricted tier, an AI can help with the first pass,
under the same rules:

- it starts fresh for each tier, sees only that tier's lists and the material, and is
  discarded afterwards,
- it never carries anything into open work or into another tier, and
- everything it sends back goes through a filter that lets through the three answers
  and nothing else.

Some information does travel with an answer: "hold back" tells you that part matters to
someone. That's a small disclosure, and the kit keeps it small by pointing at passages
rather than words, and by sending repeated checks of the same passage to a person.

## Where it's kept

Your community can keep each tier in a separate place with its own access, such as a
separate repository. The kit reads each tier from where you keep it and never copies one
tier's material into another's place. How material moves between tiers is up to your
community's own process.
