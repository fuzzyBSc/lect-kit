# Keeping your material safe

**In short:** keep a history of every change, keep more than one copy in more than one
place, use formats that will still open in fifty years, and make sure more than one
person knows how to look after it all.

This is the design. It isn't built yet.

## What needs protecting most

Not everything is equally precious.

- **The original record:** recordings, page images, transcripts, letters and anything
  else people said or wrote. Some of it may be the only copy anywhere. This matters
  most.
- **Your community's own work:** the model, corrections, classification decisions,
  conversation records, labels and settings. These take years to build.
- **What the kit builds from them:** tables, practice, the database and search. These
  can all be rebuilt from the first two, so losing them is an inconvenience, not a loss.

If an archive holds the originals, such as AIATSIS for many recordings, keep a note of
what they hold and where. Your copies are working copies, and theirs may be the preservation copy.

## Keeping track of changes

Every change to your material should be recorded: who made it, when, and why. That lets
anyone see how the work got to where it is, and put something back if a change was a
mistake.

The kit keeps that history by default. Old versions aren't erased, and nothing is
changed without a record. A change can be undone by making a new change that restores
what was there, so the history stays whole.

Some communities like a second person to look over changes before they're kept. You can
turn that on for any kind of material, and it suits classification decisions and
anything going out to the public.

If material is withdrawn, the history shows that it was withdrawn and by whom. Whether
the withdrawn material itself is removed from old versions is your community's
decision.

## Backups

A good rule of thumb is three copies, on two different kinds of storage, with one kept
somewhere else. For example, one on the computer you work on, one on an external drive,
and one at another trusted person's home or office.

Some things to watch:

- **Restricted material stays restricted in backups.** Each tier's backups are kept by
  people allowed to see that tier. Tiers closed to each other are backed up separately.
  Encrypt backups that leave your hands.
- **Cloud backup is an outside service.** Putting material in a cloud backup sends it to
  that company. Treat it like any other outside service: check their terms, including
  whether they use your data for AI training, and decide which tiers may go there.
- **Check that backups work.** A backup you've never restored from might not work.
  Restore from it now and then and check that everything comes back.
- **Withdrawn material may still be in old backups.** Decide what you'll do about it,
  and record it.

## Formats that last

The kit keeps your material in plain formats: text files and common image and audio
formats. You can open them without the kit.

The kit also builds databases to make searching and building fast, but those are
temporary. They're always rebuilt from your files, so they never need backing up, and
nothing is ever kept only in a database. If the kit stops
being looked after, or a company disappears, your material is still readable.

Keep original recordings and images in their original form, alongside any copies made
for everyday use.

## More than one keeper

Work like this can outlast any one person's involvement. Make sure:

- more than one trusted person can get to each tier's material and backups,
- passwords and keys are held in a way your community has agreed on, not by one person
  alone,
- the steps to restore from a backup are written down where those people can find them,
  and
- someone else could pick the work up if they needed to.

## Checking it's all still there

Files can be damaged quietly, especially on old drives. The kit can keep a list of
every file with a fingerprint of its contents, and check now and then that nothing has
changed unexpectedly. If something has, you'll know which file, and you can restore it
from a backup.
