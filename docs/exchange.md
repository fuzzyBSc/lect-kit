# Sharing with related languages

**In short:** the custodians of related languages can send each other their models, on
their own terms, and use them to find possible cognates. A possible cognate is a reason
for people to talk, within your community and with your neighbours. The kit helps you
get ready for those conversations and keeps a record of what people decide.

This is the design. It isn't built yet.

## Why share

Neighbouring languages often fill each other's gaps. A word your neighbours recorded can
tell you where to look in your own record, or explain a form you couldn't account for.
Sharing models makes that comparison easier for both sides.

But a list of matching words doesn't make a language stronger by itself. People do, by
talking: Elders and speakers remembering how a word was used, families comparing what
they were told, custodians of neighbouring languages working out what they share and
where they differ. The kit is there to give those conversations something to start
from.

## Sending

Your community decides what to send, to whom, and on what terms. A package holds your
model for the tiers you choose to share, with a note of:

- who sent it and when,
- which version of your model it is,
- your terms of use, and
- who to contact with questions.

You can include a summary of which forms are recorded and how often. Recordings and
transcripts are never in a package. If you want to share those, you do it your own way.

The kit prepares the package and checks it the same way it checks any build. A person
from your community looks it over and sends it. The kit never sends anything by itself.

## Receiving

A package you receive stays your neighbours'. The kit keeps it beside your own model,
labelled with who sent it, the date and their terms. You can look through it and see its
tables. Nothing from it goes into your own model or your own measures, and you don't
publish it unless their terms allow it and you decide to.

## Possible cognates

With two models side by side, the kit can suggest words that might be cognates, based
on:

- similar meanings,
- forms that differ in the ways a known sound correspondence predicts, and
- shared endings or sentence patterns.

Each suggestion comes with the reasons behind it. A suggestion isn't a finding. It's a
question for people who know the languages.

## Talking it through

A possible cognate usually raises questions only people can answer. Does the word mean
the same thing in both places? Is it used the same way? Did people on both sides know
it? Is it the same word, or a borrowing, or a coincidence? Is there anything about it
that one community would rather not share?

The kit can help you prepare. For a possible cognate, or a group of them, it can put
together a short sheet for a conversation:

- the two words side by side, with what each community's record and model hold,
- why the kit suggested them,
- the questions that are still open, and
- anything either side has marked as needing care.

You might use a sheet in your own community first, with Elders, speakers, families and
learners, before taking it to your neighbours. Or you might sit down with your
neighbours' custodians and work through several together. How, when and with whom those
conversations happen is up to the communities involved. The kit doesn't arrange them and
can't stand in for them.

Afterwards, the kit can keep a record of what was decided, in each community's own
words: who took part (as you choose to record it), what was agreed, what was left open,
and what each side wants to look into further. A possible cognate that people decided
against is remembered, with the reason, so it doesn't come back. One that's still being
talked about stays open.

Nothing changes in either community's model because of a suggestion alone. A change
comes from what people decide.

## Using what you learn

A cognate can show you where to look in your own record. The kit shows what your own
record and model hold for the word. If you decide to add a word or rule to your model
because of a cognate, the kit notes where the idea came from and which conversation it
came out of. It never counts as evidence for your language. Only your own record can do
that.

If you notice something your neighbours might want to know, such as a likely gap or a
possible error, the kit can help you write it up as a note to them. What they do with it
is up to them.
