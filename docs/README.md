# lect-kit guide

These pages explain what lect-kit does and why, for language custodians, community
members, teachers and anyone thinking of using the kit. You don't need to read code to
follow them.

Start with the [operating principles](../PRINCIPLES.md). They say what the kit is for and
what stays in a community's hands.

## Pages

- [How the kit works](workflow.md): from recordings to tables, practice and checking.
- [Classification](classification.md): keeping restricted knowledge with the people it
  belongs to.
- [Cultural and intellectual property](icip.md): keeping your rights in your language,
  taking material back, and deciding where it goes.
- [Using AI, or not](ai.md): working without AI, and keeping your knowledge out of AI
  training if you do use it.
- [Sharing with related languages](exchange.md): exchanging models, finding possible
  cognates, and the conversations they lead to.
- [Keeping your material safe](keeping-safe.md): tracking changes, backups, formats
  that last, and more than one keeper.
- [What goes in this repository](content_rules.md): why the kit holds none of your
  language.
- [The demo](../examples/demo/README.md): why the examples use Japanese.

To see what each part of the kit is meant to do, read the files in `features/`. Each
one says who a part is for, what it does and why, and lists the behaviours someone could
check by hand. Every test and every piece of code in the kit answers one of them.

Agents working on the kit keep their own notes in `knowledge/`. You're welcome to look,
but those notes are written for agents, not as reading.

## Words used in these pages

**Corpus.** Everything your community holds of what speakers said: recordings,
transcripts, letters, word lists, old books.

**Paradigm, or model.** A description of how the language builds its words: which words
there are, how they change, and which endings go together. It's a set of claims, and the
corpus tests them.

**Attested.** A form someone was recorded saying or writing.

**Built.** A form the model predicts that isn't in the corpus. It may be perfectly good
language that nobody happened to record.

**Lect.** A language, under the name its speakers or community give it.

**Tier.** A level of access a community sets, such as material open to everyone or
material for certain people only.

**Audience.** Who a particular build of the kit is for, meaning which tiers they may
see.
